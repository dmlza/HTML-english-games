Essential Rules
Word Order
Basic English sentence structure
Pattern: Subject + Verb + Object
Examples: I eat apples. She reads books.
Using a/an/the
Articles go before nouns
a: before consonants (a cat)
an: before vowels (an apple)
the: for specific things
Making Plurals
How to make words plural
Regular: cat → cats, box → boxes
Irregular: child → children, foot → feet
Present Tense
Actions happening now or regularly
I/You/We/They: walk, eat, go
He/She/It: walks, eats, goes (add -s)
Past Tense
Actions that happened before
Regular: walk → walked, play → played
Irregular: go → went, eat → ate
Making Questions
How to ask questions
Present: Do you like coffee?
Past: Did you go home?
Making Negatives
How to say no or not
Present: I don't like coffee
Past: I didn't go
Using "ing"
Actions happening right now
Pattern: am/is/are + verb-ing
Examples: I am walking. She is eating.
Adjective Position
Where to put describing words
Before: red car, big house
After 'be': The car is red
Future Tense
Actions that will happen later
will: I will go tomorrow
going to: I am going to study

Next Level Rules
Comparing Things
Making comparisons
-er/-est: big → bigger → biggest
more/most: beautiful → more beautiful
Possessives
Showing ownership
With 's: John's car, cat's food
Pronouns: my, your, his, her, its
in/on/at
Prepositions for time and place
Time: at 5pm, on Monday, in January
Place: at home, on table, in box
Can/Could/Should
Modal verbs for ability, advice
Can: I can swim (ability)
Should: You should study (advice)
If Sentences
Talking about possibilities
Real: If it rains, I will stay home
Unreal: If I were rich, I would travel
There is/are
Talking about existence
Singular: There is a cat
Plural: There are many books
Some/Any/Much/Many
Words for amounts and quantities
Things you can count: many books, some apples
Things you can't count: much water, some rice
Frequency Adverbs
How often something happens
Order: always, usually, often, sometimes, never
Position: I always brush my teeth
Question Words
Who, what, where, when, why, how
Who: Who is that? (person)
What: What do you want? (thing)
Where: Where are you going? (place)
Relative Clauses
Connecting ideas with who, which, that
Who: The man who lives here
Which: The book which I bought
That: The car that is red
