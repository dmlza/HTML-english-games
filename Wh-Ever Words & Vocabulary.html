Warm Up ☀️

Get the conversation started before we dive in. No right or wrong answers here!

🎯 Teacher Note Give students 2–3 minutes to think, then share with a partner before opening to the class. These are designed to activate background knowledge and get students comfortable speaking.
Warm-Up Question 1
Think of a time you had to do something nerve-wracking for the first time. What was it? How did you feel before, during, and after?
Example
"The first time I had to speak in front of my whole class, I was terrified. My hands were shaking and I kept forgetting what I wanted to say. But afterward, I actually felt really proud — it wasn't as bad as I imagined."

💬 Follow-up prompts: Did it get easier the second time? What helped you get through it?
Warm-Up Question 2
If you had to choose between these two situations, which would make you more nervous — and why?

🎤 Giving a speech to 100 strangers   OR   🍽️ Having dinner with your partner's parents for the first time
Things to Think About
Speech: One-way pressure — you just perform. Strangers have no long-term impact on your life.

Partner's parents: Two-way interaction — they ask questions, judge you, AND you'll probably see them again for years. Higher personal stakes!

💬 Language to encourage: "I'd rather… because…" / "For me, the scariest part would be…"
Warm-Up Question 3
What's one piece of advice you'd give to someone who always worries too much about what other people think?
Example
"I'd tell them that most people are too busy thinking about themselves to notice your mistakes. And whoever judges you harshly probably isn't someone whose opinion matters anyway."

💡 Notice the Wh-ever word snuck in there — a nice preview of today's grammar!

The Wh-Ever Words

Use these words to mean "it doesn't matter which / who / where / when / how / what" — they express total openness or indifference.

Quick Reference

Whatever Thing / action / idea "Whatever you say will be fine."
Whoever Person "Whoever calls, I'm busy."
Wherever Place / location "Wherever you go, I'll follow."
Whenever Time "Whenever you're ready, let's go."
However Manner / way "However you do it is fine."
Whichever Choosing from a set of options "Whichever seat you like."
🔑 The Key Question to Ask Ask yourself: what is the blank referring to?
A person? → Whoever  |  A place? → Wherever  |  A time? → Whenever
A thing/idea? → Whatever  |  A way/method? → However  |  Picking from options? → Whichever
⚠️ Whatever vs. Whichever Both refer to things, but Whichever implies a limited set of choices is available ("whichever shirt" = new one or old one). Whatever implies no limit at all ("whatever you say" = anything at all).
⚠️ However ≠ "but" In this grammar, however means "in whatever way / no matter how" — it is NOT the linking word meaning "but/nevertheless". Compare:
✅ "However you dress, you'll look great." (= no matter how)
✅ "I was tired. However, I finished." (= but / linking word — different use!)

Vocabulary 2

Choose the answer that best completes each sentence. Use the vocabulary box as your guide.

1My boss asked me to ________ some of the new employees how to use our computers.
a explain b train c describe d notice
✓ Correct Answer
b — train
The vocabulary box includes the phrase "train some new employees" as a fixed expression. "Train" means to teach someone a specific skill for a job — exactly what a boss asks when introducing new staff to company systems.
a – explain: You explain facts or ideas, but not people. You can't "explain employees".
c – describe: Means to give details about appearance or characteristics — wrong action here.
d – notice: Means to observe/see something — completely wrong grammar and meaning here.
2Whatever you do, don't attend a high school ________ and expect everyone to look exactly the same.
a organization b placement c registration d reunion
✓ Correct Answer
d — reunion
The vocabulary box lists "attend a high school reunion" — a reunion is an event where people who went to school together meet again after years apart. The sentence's context (people have changed since school) perfectly matches this word.
a – organization: Refers to a group/company structure — not an event you attend.
b – placement: A test to determine your level (e.g., placement exam) — unrelated here.
c – registration: The process of signing up — not the right event noun.
3How does the audience usually react when you give a ________ at a sales meeting?
a presentation b presenter c present d prescription
✓ Correct Answer
a — presentation
From the vocabulary box: "give a presentation at a sales meeting". A presentation is the noun form of the act of presenting. "Give a presentation" is the natural fixed phrase in English.
b – presenter: A person who presents — you can't "give a presenter".
c – present: Either a gift or a verb — "give a present" means giving a gift, not a business talk.
d – prescription: A medical/pharmacy term — completely unrelated to sales meetings.
4Wherever you ________ with your boyfriend, it's going to be uncomfortable.
a break up b break down c break out d break apart
✓ Correct Answer
a — break up
The vocabulary box lists "break up with someone" — the standard phrasal verb for ending a romantic relationship. The sentence context (boyfriend, uncomfortable) confirms this is about ending a relationship.
b – break down: Means to stop functioning (car breaks down) or to cry/collapse emotionally — not "end a relationship".
c – break out: Means to escape, or for something to suddenly start (war breaks out, acne breaks out).
d – break apart: Not a standard fixed phrasal verb in this context — it means to physically fall into pieces.
5Would you rather ________ in a play or see one as an audience member?
a present b perform c attend d train
✓ Correct Answer
b — perform
From the vocabulary box: "perform in a play". The sentence itself gives the contrast: performing (acting on stage) vs. watching as an audience member. "Perform in" is the fixed collocation for acting in a theatrical production.
a – present: "Present in a play" is not a natural phrase — presents are gifts, or you present data/awards.
c – attend: Attend means to go and watch — which is exactly what the audience member does. The question sets these as opposites, so "attend" would create a contradiction.
d – train: You train employees, not in plays.

Grammar 2

Choose the correct Wh-word. Ask yourself: what type of thing does the blank refer to?

1[However / Whatever / Whenever] my son wants to be in the future is fine with us.
✓ Correct Answer
Whatever
The blank refers to a thing / role / career — "a doctor, a teacher, an artist…" That's a thing, so we use Whatever. The whole clause "Whatever my son wants to be" acts as the subject of the sentence.
However: Refers to manner/method — "in whatever way". Doesn't fit here (his future career isn't a method).
Whenever: Refers to time. The sentence isn't about when — it's about what he becomes.
2[Whichever / Wherever / Whoever] university you choose to go to will be great!
✓ Correct Answer
Whichever
The person is choosing a university from a limited set of options they're considering. Whichever is used when selecting from a defined group of choices — "whichever university" implies there are a few on the table.
Wherever: Refers to place. A university isn't a location being chosen — it's an institution (an option).
Whoever: Refers to a person. A university is not a person.
3[Whatever / However / Whenever] you decide to give your notice to the company, let me know.
✓ Correct Answer
Whenever
The blank refers to time — "let me know when you decide (at any point in the future)." Whenever = "at whatever time." The sentence is about timing the decision, not what the decision is or how it's made.
Whatever: Would make it about the thing/action decided — "whatever you decide to give" — which changes and distorts the meaning.
However: Refers to manner — how you give notice. But "let me know" suggests they care about timing, not method.
4[Whatever / However / Whenever] you choose to go there, it's going to take a long time.
✓ Correct Answer
However
The blank refers to method/manner of travel — plane, bus, car, train… "In whatever way you travel, it will take long." However = "in whatever way / no matter how." The focus is on the mode of getting there.
Whatever: Would refer to a thing. "Whatever you choose to go there" is slightly awkward — "go there" implies movement method, not a thing to choose.
Whenever: Refers to time. The sentence isn't about when the trip happens — it's about the method.
5[Whoever / Whichever / Whatever] you marry is your business.
✓ Correct Answer
Whoever
You marry a person. Whoever = "any person / no matter who." The clause "Whoever you marry" = "any person you choose to marry." Always use Whoever when the blank refers to a human being.
Whichever: Used for choosing between a set of non-person options. People aren't "options in a set" in this context.
Whatever: Refers to things/ideas — not people. Using "whatever" for a person sounds dehumanizing and is grammatically wrong here.
6[Whatever / Whoever / Wherever] you go I'll follow.
✓ Correct Answer
Wherever
The blank refers to a place — "no matter which city / country / location you go to." Wherever = "in / to whatever place." "You go" is a movement verb, which confirms we're talking about a destination (place).
Whatever: Refers to things. "Whatever you go" is grammatically incorrect — you go to a place, not a thing.
Whoever: Refers to people. "You go" does not refer to a person.

Conversation Fill-in

Complete the conversation with the correct Wh-ever word: whenever, however, whoever, whatever, whichever.

KellyLucas! Hi! How are you?
LucasHonestly? I'm nervous. I'm meeting my partner's parents this evening.
KellyThat's exciting.
LucasI'm not looking forward to it. What if they don't like me? I'm worried that I'll say something wrong.
KellyDon't worry. ¹ ___ you say will be great.
LucasI'm serious, Kelly, this is a big deal. I even bought a new shirt for this evening.
KellyYou're funny. New shirt or old shirt… ² ___ shirt you wear will be OK.
LucasI don't believe that.
KellyListen. It's normal to be nervous ³ ___ you meet someone for the first time.
LucasSo you're saying ⁴ ___ I dress or ⁵ ___ I say won't make a difference.
KellyI'm saying that they're probably nervous about meeting you, too.
LucasWhat?
Kelly⁶ ___ you meet will like you… if you don't try to be someone else.
LucasSo just be myself.
KellyExactly. Just be Lucas.
✓ All Answers
¹ Whatever
Refers to a thing/words Lucas might say — "any thing you say." No limit on what the thing is → Whatever.
² Whichever
Kelly just listed two specific options: "new shirt or old shirt." When choosing between a defined set → Whichever.
³ Whenever
Refers to time — "at any point when you meet someone new." Movement + time context → Whenever.
⁴ However
"However I dress" = in whatever manner/way I dress. The verb "dress" is about method/manner → However.
⁵ Whatever
"Whatever I say" = any words / thing I say. Refers to the content of speech (a thing) → Whatever.
⁶ Whoever
"Whoever you meet" = any person you meet. Refers to the parents/people → Whoever (always for people).

Speaking Practice

Practice giving encouragement. Use Wh-ever words naturally in your responses!

💡 Tip for students: Try to use at least one Wh-ever word when giving encouragement. For example: "Whatever happens, you'll do great!" or "Whoever interviews you will be impressed."
Scenario 1
😰 Your friend is nervous about quitting a stressful job.
Example Response
"Whatever happens after you quit, you'll find something better. Whenever you're ready to make the move, I'll support you. No job is worth your health!"

🔑 Whatever (thing = outcomes) + Whenever (time = when they're ready)
Scenario 2
😰 Your friend has to give a speech in front of a large group.
Example Response
"Whatever you say up there, the audience will appreciate your effort. However nervous you feel, just breathe and speak from the heart."

🔑 Whatever (thing = words spoken) + However (manner = how nervous)
Scenario 3
😰 Your colleague is presenting the annual sales report at a meeting.
Example Response
"Whoever is in that room will be impressed. You know this data better than anyone. However the questions come, you'll handle them."

🔑 Whoever (people = the audience) + However (manner = how questions are asked)
Scenario 4
😰 Your manager has to fire 20 employees because sales have decreased.
Example Response
"This isn't your fault. Whatever decision you make, do it with kindness. Whoever you have to let go, treat them with respect — that's what they'll remember."

🔑 Whatever (thing = the decision) + Whoever (people = the employees)
Scenario 5
😰 Your friend is nervous about attending their high school reunion.
Example Response
"Whoever you run into there, just be yourself. Whatever they think about how you look now doesn't matter — you're happy, and that shows."

🔑 Whoever (people = old classmates) + Whatever (thing = their opinions)
Scenario 6
😰 Your friend is nervous about meeting their partner's parents for the first time.
Example Response
"Whoever they are, they raised someone amazing — and that means they're good people. Whatever you say tonight, just be honest and warm. They'll love you."

🔑 Whoever (people = the parents) + Whatever (thing = what is said)

Vocabulary 📚

Key words and phrases from today's lesson. Read the meaning and example, then write your own sentence!

🎯 Your Task After reading each card, write your own sentence using the word or phrase. Try to connect it to something real in your life!
adjective nervous ▼
Meaning: Feeling anxious, worried, or uneasy about something that might happen.
"I'm nervous. I'm meeting my partner's parents this evening."
✍️ Your turn: When do you usually feel nervous?
phrase not look forward to (something) ▼
Meaning: To feel no excitement or even dread about a future event. The opposite of "look forward to." Often used when something feels like an obligation.
"I'm not looking forward to it. What if they don't like me?"
✍️ Your turn: Is there anything coming up that you're not looking forward to?
idiom a big deal ▼
Meaning: Something very important or significant. Often used to emphasise how seriously someone takes a situation — or sarcastically, to say it isn't important.
"I'm serious, Kelly, this is a big deal. I even bought a new shirt for this evening."
✍️ Your turn: What's something that felt like a big deal to you recently?
phrasal verb run into (someone) ▼
Meaning: To meet someone unexpectedly and by chance — you didn't plan it. Common in everyday speech.
"Kelly is shopping in the supermarket when she runs into her friend Lucas."
✍️ Your turn: Have you ever run into someone unexpectedly? What happened?
phrase make a good impression ▼
Meaning: To cause someone to think well of you — often when meeting for the first time. You can also "make a bad impression" or "make a lasting impression."
"I get most nervous when I want to make a good impression on someone new."
✍️ Your turn: Think of a time you really wanted to make a good impression. Did it go well?
verb calm (someone) down ▼
Meaning: To become less upset, anxious, or excited — or to help someone else do this. Can be used reflexively: "I calmed myself down."
"I try to take deep breaths to calm myself down when I feel anxious."
✍️ Your turn: What do you do to calm yourself down when you're stressed?
phrase give your notice ▼
Meaning: To formally tell your employer that you are leaving your job. Usually requires a set period (e.g., two weeks' notice). Also: "hand in your notice" / "put in your notice."
"Whenever you decide to give your notice to the company, let me know."
✍️ Your turn: Have you or someone you know ever given notice at a job? How did it feel?
adjective genuine ▼
Meaning: Truly what it appears to be; authentic and sincere. Used for people, emotions, or things. A "genuine person" is honest and not fake.
"People usually respond better when you're genuine."
✍️ Your turn: Describe someone in your life who you think is genuinely kind. What makes them that way?
verb pretend ▼
Meaning: To act as if something is true when it isn't — to put on a false appearance. Used for emotions, identities, or situations.
"Pretending to be someone else is exhausting."
✍️ Your turn: Have you ever had to pretend to be fine when you weren't? What was the situation?
adjective exhausting ▼
Meaning: Making you feel very tired — physically or mentally. Something can be physically exhausting (running a marathon) or emotionally exhausting (worrying all day).
"Pretending to be someone else is exhausting."
✍️ Your turn: What's the most mentally exhausting thing you deal with regularly?
phrase reassurance ▼
Meaning: Words or actions that remove someone's doubts or fears and make them feel more confident. The verb form is "to reassure (someone)."
"Even simple reassurance can really help someone relax before a stressful event."
✍️ Your turn: Write a sentence of reassurance you could say to a nervous friend.
phrase make a difference ▼
Meaning: To have a noticeable effect or impact on a situation or person. Often used in questions and negatives: "It won't make a difference" / "Does it really make a difference?"
"So you're saying however I dress or whatever I say won't make a difference."
✍️ Your turn: Do you think first impressions really make a difference? Why or why not?

Discussion Questions 💭

These are open-ended — there are no wrong answers. Sample answers are provided as a guide, not a script.

🎯 Teacher Note Encourage students to expand their answers using Wh-ever words! For example: "Whatever happens, I just try to…" or "Whoever I'm with, I feel nervous when…"
Question 1
What kinds of situations usually make you feel the most nervous?
Model Answer
"I get most nervous when I have to meet new people, especially if I want to make a good impression. I tend to overthink whatever I'll say."

💬 Follow-ups: Does it depend on who the person is? Does meeting someone online first make it easier?
Question 2
How do you usually calm yourself down when you feel anxious?
Model Answer
"I try to take deep breaths and remind myself that the situation won't last forever. Sometimes listening to music helps, too."

💬 Follow-ups: Do you have a go-to song for when you're stressed? Does the method change depending on the situation?
Question 3
Do you think being yourself is always the best advice in stressful social situations? Why or why not?
Model Answer
"I think it's good advice because pretending to be someone else is exhausting. People usually respond better when you're genuine. However, in some formal situations, you still need to adjust your tone — that's not being fake, that's being professional."

💬 Follow-ups: Is there a difference between "being yourself" and "being professional"? Can both coexist?
Question 4
Has worrying about making a bad impression ever turned out to be unnecessary for you?
Model Answer
"Yes, many times. I often realize afterward that people were much kinder and more understanding than I expected. Whatever I'd been dreading usually never happened."

💬 Follow-ups: Why do you think we tend to expect the worst? Is that a human thing, or is it cultural?
Question 5
How comfortable are you with giving encouragement to someone who is anxious?
Model Answer
"I'm fairly comfortable because I know how anxiety feels. Even simple reassurance can really help someone relax. Whoever is struggling, I try to remind them that it will pass."

💬 Follow-ups: Is there a phrase or sentence you always say to encourage someone? Where did you learn it?

Would You Rather? 🎲

End-of-lesson fun. No wrong answers — but you must choose one and explain why using today's language!

🎯 Challenge for Students You must use at least one Wh-ever word in your answer. Try: "Whatever I choose, I think…" or "Whoever is in the situation would probably…"
Hypothetical 1 😂
Would you rather have to perform a dramatic one-person play at your partner's family dinner every single time you visit — OR — give a 10-minute work presentation while your entire family watches from the back of the room?
Things to Discuss
🎭 Play at dinner: Your partner's family sees the most embarrassing version of you — every visit, forever. But at least it becomes a tradition and whoever meets you knows what to expect!

💼 Work presentation with family: Your mum is in the back row making proud faces while your boss is asking hard questions. One time only — but the memories last forever.

💬 Sample answer using Wh-ever: "I'd rather do the work presentation because however embarrassing it is, it only happens once. Whatever my family does in the back row, I can ignore it."
Hypothetical 2 😂
Would you rather accidentally call your teacher "Mum" or "Dad" in front of the whole class — OR — trip and fall on the way to receive an award in front of 500 people?
Things to Discuss
👩‍🏫 "Mum" slip: Deeply personal, lingers in classroom memory for the rest of the year. Whoever is in that class will bring it up at every reunion.

🏆 Award trip: 500 witnesses, but it's a one-second moment. You got an award — that's still a win. Whatever people remember, you still went home with the trophy.

💬 Sample answer: "I'd rather trip at the award ceremony. However embarrassing it is, at least everyone knows I won something. Whenever I think about calling my teacher 'Mum', I want to disappear."
Hypothetical 3 😂
Would you rather have to text your ex every time you use a Wh-ever word in real life — OR — have your phone announce your grammar mistakes out loud in a robot voice whenever you're in a quiet public place?
Things to Discuss
💬 Text your ex: You'd either stop using Wh-ever words completely (bad for your English!) or your ex gets a LOT of random messages that just say "whatever" with no context.

🤖 Robot voice: Imagine sitting in a library and your phone screams: "INCORRECT — HOWEVER IS FOR MANNER, NOT TIME." Humiliating, but your English would improve extremely fast.

💬 Sample answer: "I'd rather have the robot voice. Whoever is around me would just have to deal with it. Whatever embarrassment I feel is worth it — at least I'm learning!"
